The failure a timestamp watermark misses

A January invoice for £50 and payment for £40 arrive after the April corrections. Their recorded timestamps are older than the last timestamps already loaded. A max(recorded_at) filter would skip both.

Single-writer committed ingestion sequence → incremental revision tables → cutoff-selected views → customer/month and invoice marts → shared metric → management scorecard

The sequence-based load retains both records. January's balance increases by £10, which carries into later months. Rebuilding the entire history produces exactly the same four mart row sets.

A metric change with visible consequences

Version 1 defines growth as new MRR. Version 2 adds reactivation. Customer C's March return contributes £90: January and February stay unchanged, while the shared model and its dependent scorecard both move from £0 to £90 for March. The underlying MRR, invoices and cash do not change.

What this does—and does not—prove

Demonstrated

  • Real SQL execution and an independent row-level Python oracle.
  • Replay safety, rejected conflicting revision identities and a reproducible historical cutoff.
  • Late correction/backfill equivalence with a full refresh.
  • Empty months, carried balances and downstream metric-definition impact.

Explicit boundaries

  • One ordered writer; arbitrary concurrent CDC and out-of-order journal commits are unsupported.
  • Four history tables are incremental; bounded downstream views are recomputed.
  • dbt is not an atomic multi-model close; only the validated evidence bundle is published.
  • No managed cloud operation, live customer use or independent human review is claimed.
